Identity, Headquarters and General Provisions
Who We Are, and Where We Work
Takamul Association for Building Values and Development is an independent, non-profit civil society organization, registered in accordance with the law, that seeks to build people, strengthen the system of ethical and social values, and support sustainable development through distinctive and innovative projects.
Official Name
Takamul Association for Building Values and Development – a registered civil society organization with a comprehensive community and development scope of work
Headquarters
Istanbul, Republic of Türkiye
Scope of Work
Local and international; the Association may open branches and representative offices inside and outside Türkiye in accordance with the approved expansion plan

Vision, Mission and Strategic Objectives
Where We Are Heading, and How We Get There
Vision
To be the world’s leading institution in building values, entrenching moral foundations and strengthening coordination and partnership among civil society organizations, and to achieve comprehensive and sustainable social and economic development for communities, families and individuals by 2035.
Mission
To instill ethical values and skills in individuals and institutions, invest human potential through value-based and integrative programs, activate partnerships with Turkish associations and diaspora community associations, and launch model development and productive initiatives that bring stability and prosperity and create job opportunities.
2.1  Strategic Objectives
1
Promoting Positive Values
Entrenching ethical, humanitarian and moderate values in society, and raising generations with a firm values-based frame of reference.
2
Empowerment and Qualification
Equipping young people, families and women with modern life and economic skills and vocational qualification.
3
Institutional Integration and Coordination
Connecting Turkish associations and diaspora community associations to avoid duplication of effort, exchange expertise and maximize the use of resources.
4
Supporting Development and Official Efforts
Supporting the state’s efforts to provide job opportunities and vocational training and to reduce economic challenges and unemployment.
5
Sustainability and Comprehensive Solutions
Providing development, social and economic solutions based on strategic partnerships and social investment.

Main Development Initiatives and Areas of Work
Two Development Initiatives and a Supporting Track
The Association achieves its field and educational objectives through two main development initiatives, supported by a track for institutional coordination and integration.
3.1  The “Yanabee” Initiative – Intellectual and Values Building and Interactive Education
Focus area: values education, intellectual building, cultural and social communication, and adaptation.
●  Delivering values and ethics curricula and workshops for children, young people and adolescents.
●  Family and educational counseling and personal development programs to build cohesive families.
●  Promoting cultural adaptation and social coexistence through dialogue sessions and cultural events.
●  Youth forums and awareness programs to protect young people from harmful behaviors.
3.2  The “Manafea” Initiative – Solidarity, Mutual Support and Economic Empowerment
Focus area: addressing economic challenges, economic empowerment, employment, and solidarity and mutual support.
●  Vocational qualification and training for young people and women, in support of the state’s plans to provide job opportunities.
●  Platforms and networks connecting talent and skilled labor with the labor market and production institutions.
●  Supporting and financing micro-enterprises and productive families to achieve self-sufficiency.
●  Social solidarity activities and indirect assistance for families in need.
3.3  Institutional Coordination and Integration Track and Supporting Activities
Resource Integration
Connecting Turkish associations and diaspora community associations to hold joint activities and integrate services.
Technical Support
Providing technical support and development training for vulnerable and needy groups.
Research and Studies
Field studies on the labor market and social conditions, and publishing awareness toolkits and magazines.
General Mandate
Carrying out social, cultural, educational, economic and sports activities, establishing economic enterprises, benefiting from grants, and signing cooperation protocols.

Why Join the “Takamul” Association?
And What Sets It Apart from Others
Joining the “Takamul Association for Building Values and Development” is not merely joining a traditional civil society organization; it is a commitment to a unique development model carefully crafted by a select group of pioneers in volunteer and humanitarian work, to be a hallmark and a turning point in the course of community work.
The Association’s strength is embodied in ten strategic pillars that make it an exceptional environment for giving and sustainable impact:
1
Leadership and Governance
A Select Group of Field and International Expertise
The Association applies strict and precise selection criteria for founding members, members of the Board of Directors and the Supervisory Board, and activity leads, to ensure professional institutional leadership. Its leaders are distinguished by the following:
●  High competence and deep accumulated experience in project management and development.
●  Sufficient time to give, and an extensive network of strategic relationships locally and internationally.
●  Good character and outstanding leadership abilities.
●  Fluency in Turkish and familiarity with the legislative and cultural environment in Türkiye.
2
Diversity and International Integration
A Board of Directors That Reflects the Spirit of the Ummah
Embodying the name “Takamul” (integration), the Board of Directors is made up of 7 members of multiple nationalities, giving the Association cultural depth and a comprehensive vision that transcends borders:
Türkiye
Syria
Palestine
Iraq
Egypt
3
Coordinating and Networking Role
A Platform for Maximizing Resources and Expertise
The Association acts as a comprehensive coordinating body and a bridge of communication between diaspora community associations and Turkish associations, with the aim of:
●  Maximizing the use of available resources and exchanging field expertise.
●  Preventing duplication of effort and overlapping projects.
●  Creating a positive competitive environment that yields continuous improvement in performance and in the services provided to the community.
4
Inclusiveness and Scope of Impact
From Istanbul to the World
The Association’s activities are not limited to a particular province or a specific community; its services extend to everyone living on Turkish soil, citizens, residents and diaspora communities of all backgrounds, and it is expanding according to a phased strategic plan to reach many countries around the world.
5
Social and Economic Security
Projects That Address Real Needs
“Takamul” goes beyond temporary relief roles to offer fundamental solutions that every individual needs to live in security and stability, through two development tracks:
The “Yanabee” Project
Social security and intellectual and values-based protection for children and families.
The “Manafea” Project
Economic and job security through training, vocational qualification, and the provision of job and employment opportunities.
6
Digital Transformation and Artificial Intelligence
Smart Management and Precise Impact Measurement
The Association relies on modern tools through an integrated technology program to manage its activities, drawing on graduates in programming, technology and artificial intelligence, which provides:
●  Smart management of field and administrative operations.
●  Precise and continuous measurement of the performance and development impact of the Association’s projects on beneficiaries.
7
Investing in Future Generations
Linking Academia with Young Leadership
The Association pays special attention to involving faculty members and academics in the Board of Directors and in managing activities, so that they serve as:
●  A solid bridge for attracting and mentoring young talent and skills from among students.
●  An incubator for preparing and qualifying these young people to become active members and future leaders of the Association and society.
8
Institutional Renewal and Leadership Rotation
Sustainability and Passing On Expertise
The Association upholds sound governance that prevents stagnation and ensures an infusion of new blood, through a clear policy on leadership terms:
Board of Directors
May not be renewed for more than one additional term; each term lasts 4 years.
Activity and Committee Leads
One term only, lasting 3 years.
Former Board members and activity leads remain advisors whose expertise the Association draws on in guiding new members; this is a core principle for passing on institutional work, selecting the best talent, and continuous innovation and development.
9
Full-Time Administrative and Executive Dedication
Greater Focus and Faster Impact
To ensure effectiveness and the swift achievement of field objectives, the Association has adopted a full-time model for 5 members of the founding team, providing a dedicated executive secretariat that manages activities and follows up on their impact in the community with high efficiency.
10
Sustainability and Financial Independence
A Smart Self-Financing Model
The founding body has developed a robust financial plan that ensures the institution’s strength and continuity, independent of fluctuations in donations:
Founding Coverage
In its first two years, the Association relies on the financial capacity of its founding members to fund activities and programs.
Developmental Social Investment
Establishing economic projects and a smart digital application (the “Manafea” app) to serve as a sustainable financial resource that permanently funds the Association’s activities and programs.
Joining “Takamul” means taking part in a well-established development experience: a robust model, expert leadership and guaranteed sustainability. Hand in hand, let us build people and achieve sustainable development!

Administrative and Strategic Answer Guide
Key Questions Founders and the Board of Directors Need to Address
13.1  Questions from Founding Members (Founders)
Foundations, Vision, Governance, Sustainability and Risks
Q1
Why does this institution exist now? What is the core problem we seek to solve, and what unique value do we offer?
Administrative Response
The institution exists to address a real development and social gap, namely the fragmentation of efforts and the lack of integration between Turkish civil society organizations and diaspora community associations, alongside the pressing need for a model that combines values-based development with sustainable economic qualification.
Our added value lies in achieving “Dual Impact” through the “Yanabee” initiative for educational and values building and the “Manafea” initiative for economic empowerment and employment, while serving as a comprehensive coordinating platform that provides resources and prevents duplicate initiatives.
Q2
What is our long-term vision (5–10 years)? And how do we define “success”?
Administrative Response
Strategic vision (2035): Achieving global leadership in building values and empowering communities in humanitarian and developmental terms.
Concept of success: Measured through comprehensive key performance indicators (KPIs):
Social and Values Impact
Raising generations with strong cohesion in values and upbringing.
Economic Impact
Transforming beneficiaries from aid recipients into independent productive forces.
Geographic Expansion
Implementing the approved phased plan from Istanbul to the Turkish provinces, the Islamic countries and the world.
Self-Sustainability
Funding at least 50% of the operating budget through economic enterprises and social investment.
Q3
What core values (Core Values) and ethics will govern the work culture?
Administrative Response
The institution’s culture rests on five ethical and institutional pillars:
●  Integration and connection (Synergy): Cooperation and preventing duplication of effort.
●  Empowerment and continuity (Empowerment): Focusing on building people rather than temporary care.
●  Transparency and governance (Transparency): Full financial and administrative disclosure.
●  Social innovation (Innovation): Providing flexible and modern development solutions.
●  Responsibility and moderation (Accountability): Commitment to the highest standards of professional and social integrity.
Q4
What legal entity and legislative frameworks are best suited to our activity?
Administrative Response
The entity is an independent, non-profit development civil society association, registered under the provisions of the Turkish Law on Associations in Istanbul. Its bylaws include the authority to establish economic, commercial and investment enterprises and to open branches and representative offices inside and outside Türkiye, providing legal cover for all field and investment activities.
Q5
How are powers and responsibilities distributed among the different bodies at establishment?
Administrative Response
Powers are distributed on the basis of the governance charter and the bylaws:
●  General Assembly (Genel Kurul): The supreme authority for voting, adopting amendments and approving general policies.
●  Board of Directors (Yönetim Kurulu): The executive authority responsible for managing activities, establishing economic enterprises and overseeing the executive departments.
●  Supervisory Board (Denetim Kurulu): An independent body for financial and administrative auditing and oversight.
●  General Secretariat / Executive Management: Managing field operations and day-to-day activities in accordance with the delegation of authority matrix (Delegation of Authority).
Q6
What regulations and governance charter protect the founders’ rights and regulate joining and leaving?
Administrative Response
The bylaws and the governance charter are adopted, and they set out:
●  The conditions and procedures for applying for, terminating or suspending membership.
●  The non-refundability of previous subscriptions or donations, to protect the institution’s financial assets.
●  The preservation of the moral and historical rights of the founding body through the official records of the founding documents.
Q7
How do we achieve financial sustainability and avoid relying on temporary donations?
Administrative Response
Sustainability is achieved through a multi-source funding model (Hybrid Funding Model):
●  Annual subscriptions and membership fees.
●  Profits and returns from the Association’s economic and commercial enterprises (social investment).
●  Grants and partnerships with local and international funds and institutions.
●  Revenue from activities, publications and exhibitions, and donations collected in accordance with legislation.
Q8
How much operating capital is required (Burn Rate)? And when will we reach the break-even point?
Administrative Response
The estimated operating budget (OpEx) is set for each phase of expansion, and the break-even point (Break-even Point) is calculated by linking fixed operating expenses to the revenues of economic enterprises and grant partnerships, so that self-generated revenues cover fixed administrative expenses within the first 24–36 months of operation.
Q9
What are the main operational, financial and security risks? And how do we prepare mitigation plans?
Administrative Response
Risks are managed through a risk register (Risk Register):
●  Legal and compliance risks: Full adherence to the legislation on associations and to labor and residence laws.
●  Financial risks: Applying a highly liquid cash reserve policy that covers operations for at least 6 months.
●  Reputational risks: Adopting a strict media and ethics charter that safeguards the Association’s standing.
13.2  Questions from the Board of Directors (Board of Directors)
Monitoring, Performance, Financial Oversight, Compliance and Development
Q1
Is the executive management operating in line with the approved strategic plans?
Administrative Response
This is verified through quarterly monitoring reports (Quarterly Performance Reviews) submitted by the General Secretariat to the Board, which compare field outputs against the strategic targets set out in the operational plan for the “Yanabee” and “Manafea” initiatives.
Q2
What key performance indicators (KPIs) are used to measure the success of the executive management?
Administrative Response
The indicators are distributed across four perspectives according to the balanced scorecard (Balanced Scorecard):
Financial Perspective
The growth rate of self-generated revenue, and the ratio of administrative expenses to program expenses.
Beneficiary Perspective
The number of young people and families benefiting from qualification, employment and values workshops, and their level of satisfaction.
Internal Processes Perspective
The number of partnerships signed with Turkish associations and diaspora community associations, and the rate of on-time project completion.
Growth and Learning Perspective
The talent retention rate, and the development of curricula and programs.
Q3
Do we have replacement and succession plans (Succession Planning) for senior management?
Administrative Response
Yes. The Board adopts a leadership succession policy based on training the second tier of leadership in the five executive departments and documenting all procedural manuals and operating policies, ensuring continuity of work that is not tied to specific individuals.
Q4
Are the financial statements accurate, and are they subject to a rigorous independent audit?
Administrative Response
Yes. All financial transactions are subject to auditing at two levels:
●  Internal oversight: Through the Supervisory Board (Denetim Kurulu) under the provisions of the bylaws.
●  External oversight: Through accredited certified accounting firms that prepare the financial statements and conduct the statutory audit in accordance with the requirements of the official authorities.
Q5
Are cash flows and approved budgets managed efficiently and without waste?
Administrative Response
Cash flows are managed under an annual estimated budget (Annual Budget) approved by the Board of Directors, and no expenditure is made except in accordance with the financial approval matrix. The financial system also requires a budget variance analysis (Variance Analysis) to be presented periodically to control any waste.
Q6
Is the institution fully compliant with legal and tax regulations, so as to shield the Board from any liability?
Administrative Response
The institution adheres to the highest levels of compliance (Compliance):
●  Registering all contracts and activities in accordance with the Law on Associations and the Turkish Labor Law.
●  Verifying the residence conditions and legal requirements for all members and staff.
●  Managing economic and commercial enterprises with independent tax and accounting records that protect Board members from any personal liability.
Q7
What are the emerging risks (market, legal, technical, reputational)? And how do we prepare contingency plans?
Administrative Response
The Board periodically reviews the risk matrix, and when any new risk arises (such as changes in legal restrictions or currency fluctuations), alternative plans are activated:
●  Digital transformation to hold meetings and seminars remotely when needed.
●  Diversifying the basket of currencies and financial resources to limit inflation risks.
●  Relying on joint cooperation protocols to reduce direct operating costs.
Q8
How do we ensure the development of services and the growth of strategic partnerships in a way that achieves sustainability?
Administrative Response
By activating the role of the Studies, Planning and Social Investment Department and the Institutional Coordination and Partnerships Department, which work to:
●  Conduct continuous field studies of the needs of the labor market and society.
●  Develop and market values and development toolkits.
●  Conclude ongoing cooperation protocols with Turkish associations, official institutions and international development funds.
